# Paper skeleton

Working title: **Antivenom as an estimator trained on a biased sample: a stoichiometric model of

## Abstract (structured, ~250 words)

India's only antivenom is raised on venom pooled from four species collected almost entirely from a
single facility in Tamil Nadu, while venom composition varies across the subcontinent. We frame this
as covariate shift and make it quantitative. We model neutralisation as a limiting-reagent problem
over a finite antibody budget rather than as geometric similarity between composition vectors, and
we pre-registered a blinded retrodiction against four published antivenom-failure findings before
fitting any parameter. The model reproduces the experimentally observed immunogen-dilution effect —
coverage has an interior maximum in mixture size and falls thereafter — with nothing fitted to
produce it, while a cosine-similarity baseline run in its strongest form is monotone non-decreasing
and cannot. One of four pre-registered targets passed; we report the three misses with computed
diagnostics and identify a single dominant cause. We further find that the kernel length scale over
which a venom proteome generalises cannot be resolved from published data: cross-validation gives a
flat optimum exceeding the north–south extent of India, meaning geographic distance currently
carries little predictive power for venom composition.

## 1. Introduction

- Snakebite as a WHO Category A neglected tropical disease; India's share of global mortality
  (Suraweera 2020, `10.7554/eLife.54076`).
- The Big Four immunogen and the Irula Cooperative as a single sourcing point.
- Established intraspecific venom variation across India (Senji Laxme 2019, 2021).
- **The gap this fills.** The literature documents variation and documents failure, but scores the
  match between antivenom and venom by geometric similarity. A geometric metric has no supply
  constraint, so it cannot represent the central experimental fact that adding immunogens can
  *reduce* efficacy.
- **Contributions.** (i) a stoichiometric coverage model; (ii) a pre-registered blinded
  retrodiction; (iii) a burden-weighted siting optimiser with a measured optimality gap; (iv) a
  negative result about the current sampling density.

## 4. Discussion

- **The framing is the contribution.** Covariate shift is a solved vocabulary; applying it here
  makes an unmeasured problem measurable.
- **The dilution result is mechanistic, not fitted**, and it has an operational corollary: adding a
  venom without re-balancing the mixture can make the product worse.
- **What the 1/4 means.** The model form survives; the cross-reactivity threshold does not. The
  diagnosis is specific and the fix is pre-registrable.
- **The sampling-density result may be the most actionable finding.** You cannot infer regional
  venom composition from what is currently published.
- **Limitations**, stated without hedging: theta unvalidated; two krait proteomes; no clinical
  validation; bite-attribution assumed; burden allocated by an area proxy; the immunogen
  composition itself interpolated because no proteome exists at the collection locality.
- **Not clinical guidance.** The output is a risk ordering.

## 5. Conclusion

What to sample, where, and how many sites — plus the honest statement of how little is currently
known, with the number attached.
