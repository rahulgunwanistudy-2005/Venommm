VenomGap

pre-registered · blinded

India’s only antivenom is raised on venom pooled from four species collected almost entirely from one facility in Tamil Nadu. Venom composition varies across the country. That is covariate shift, and covariate shift is measurable. This model treats neutralisation as a limiting-reagent problem over a finite antibody budget — not as geometric similarity — and every number here is computed in your browser from the published proteomes.

The model

Neutralisation is a limiting-reagent problem. A vial holds a fixed antibody mass B, partitioned across toxin families in proportion to the immunising mixture. Nothing below was fitted to produce the dilution effect — it follows from the supply equation.

supply_f(S,w) = B · Σ_q w_q · a_qf eff_f(S,w,p) = B · Σ_q w_q · a_qf · x_f(q→p) demand_f(p,D) = D · a_pf · κ_f n_f = min(1, eff_f / demand_f) C(p|S,w,D) = Σ_f σ_f·a_pf·n_f ⁄ Σ_f σ_f·a_pf

Non-monotone in |S| — adding a venom moves supply away from the families a bite actually delivers. Dose-dependent — demand is linear in D, supply is not. Saturating — excess antibody against a family is wasted.

What was actually fitted

One parameter. Everything else is fixed a priori or unidentifiable.

Severity weights σ_f

Fixed before any data was ingested, with a written rationale per family. Never fitted.

What this predicts, and what it does not

It predicts a risk ordering of Indian districts and venom populations by antivenom deficit; which toxin family drives each deficit; the shape of the coverage-versus-mixture-size curve; and a ranked answer to where new venom collection sites should go.

It does not predict clinical neutralisation percentages, ED50 values, vial requirements, or patient outcomes. It is not validated against clinical data and must not be used to guide treatment.
